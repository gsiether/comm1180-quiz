Week 2 · Market Opportunities
Customer Value
Customer ValueThe net benefit a customer receives: ΣBenefits − ΣCosts. Higher CV than competitors = competitive advantage.
Job to be DoneCustomers “hire” products to complete a specific task — not to own features. (e.g. customers want a hole, not a drill.)
Multi-Attribute ValueWeighted performance score: Σ(Importancei × Performancei). Used to compare perceived value across competitors.
CV= Σ Benefits − Σ Costs
Multi-Attr= Σ (Importancei × Performancei)
Higher CV than competitors = competitive advantage
“Job to be done”: customers hire products to complete a task, not just buy features
STP Framework
SegmentationDividing the market into distinct groups with similar needs (Demographic, Psychographic, Behavioural, Geographic, Firmographic).
TargetingSelecting which segment(s) to serve. Must follow segmentation — you cannot target without first defining segments.
PositioningHow the firm wants its offering perceived in the minds of the target segment relative to competitors. Must follow targeting.
Firmographic SegmentationB2B equivalent of demographics — divides markets by firm characteristics: industry, size, revenue, geography.
Segmentation → Divide market into groups (Demographic, Psychographic, Behavioural, Firmographic)
Targeting → Choose which segment(s) to serve
Positioning → How your offering is perceived vs competitors
Exam trapAlways S → T → P. Positioning cannot come before Targeting.

Smith & Colgate: 4 Types of Customer Value
FunctionalProduct performs a useful task — e.g. vacuum cleaner that cleans effectively
ExperientialCreates positive feelings/emotions — e.g. luxury spa experience
SymbolicSignals status or identity — e.g. Rolex watch
Cost/SacrificeMinimises what the customer gives up — e.g. free shipping, low price
Memory tipThink: Does it WORK? (Functional) · How does it FEEL? (Experiential) · What does it SAY about me? (Symbolic) · What does it COST me? (Cost/Sacrifice)
Economic Value Estimation (EVE)
EVE= Reference Value + Differentiation Value
Reference Value = price of the best competing alternative
Differentiation Value = monetary worth of features that differ from the competitor
EVE = the maximum a rational customer would pay before switching → sets the ceiling for value-based pricing
Exam applicationValue-based pricing sets price below EVE (to give customer a reason to buy) but above cost. The gap between price and EVE is the “value surplus” the customer keeps.
Marketing Mix — 4Ps & Value
ProductDefines what value is offered — features, quality, design
PriceCaptures value and signals quality — must reflect perceived value
PlaceMakes value accessible — channels, distribution, availability
PromotionCommunicates and persuades — advertising, sales, content
Key ideaThe 4Ps operationalise the value proposition in the market. Each P connects to creating, delivering, or capturing value — examiners expect you to link each P to the value proposition, not just list them.
Customer Value vs Stakeholder Value
Customer ValueBenefits delivered specifically to buyers
Stakeholder ValueValue created/destroyed for ALL parties — employees, community, suppliers, society
Tension example: cutting prices ↑ customer value, but funding it via wage cuts ↓ employee value
Maximising customer value alone can impose costs on other stakeholders
Exam tipAlways name a specific stakeholder and the mechanism of harm/benefit. “Stakeholders” alone without specifics earns minimal marks.
How to Approach W2 Exam Questions
Step-by-Step Guide

  2. For calculations: Multi-Attribute = Σ(Importance × Performance); EVE = Reference Value + Differentiation Value
  3. For SA questions: define the concept first, then apply it directly to the scenario
  4. Porter's: identify both dimensions (scope: broad/niche; competitive advantage: cost/unique) to name the strategy
  5. Ansoff: label product (new/existing) and market (new/existing) first → then name the quadrant
  6. VPC: check which side of the canvas (Value Map vs Customer Profile) — Gain Creators is on the Value Map, NOT the Customer Profile
  7. Always link your answer back to “value creation” — how does the strategy create/deliver/capture value?
Exam TipMarkers want application, not just definitions. If a scenario is given, always reference the specific details: “Since Lumio scores higher (182 vs 141)…” Generic answers earn minimal marks.
Week 3 · CVP Analysis — How price, volume, and cost structure drive profitability
Core Formulas
CM / unit= Selling Price − Variable Cost per unit
CM Ratio= CM per unit ÷ Selling Price
VC Ratio= VC ÷ SP → CM Ratio + VC Ratio = 1 always
Break-Even Point
BEP (units)= Fixed Costs ÷ CM per unit
BEP (revenue)= Fixed Costs ÷ CM Ratio
At BEP: Revenue = Total Cost, Profit = $0
BEP falls when: price ↑, variable cost ↓, or fixed costs ↓
Multi-Product CVP
WACM= Σ (CMi × Sales Mix %i)
Target units= (Fixed Costs + Pre-tax Profit) ÷ WACM
Pre-tax profit= After-tax Profit ÷ (1 − tax rate)
Steps1) CM each product  2) × mix %  3) Sum = WACM  4) Convert after-tax → pre-tax  5) (FC + pre-tax) ÷ WACM
Margin of Safety & Operating Leverage
MOS= Actual Sales − BEP Sales (units or $)
MOS %= MOS ÷ Actual Sales × 100
Operating leverage: High fixed costs → once BEP is passed, extra revenue is near-pure profit
Why leverage mattersHigh-FC businesses see amplified profit gains in good times and amplified losses in bad times.
How to Approach W3 Exam Questions
Step-by-Step Guide
  1. List all givens: SP, VC, FC, sales mix %, tax rate, target profit
  2. CM per product: CM = SP − VC for each product in the mix
  3. Multi-product: WACM = Σ(CMi × Mixi)
  4. BEP: FC ÷ CM (or WACM for multi-product)
  5. Target profit: if after-tax given, convert FIRST → pre-tax = after-tax ÷ (1 − tax rate), then (FC + pre-tax) ÷ CM
  6. MOS: Actual Sales − BEP; MOS% = MOS ÷ Actual Sales × 100
  7. Label units in every answer (guests, room-nights, units, $)
Most Common MistakeForgetting to convert after-tax profit to pre-tax before finding target volume. This step is compulsory whenever a tax rate is given: Pre-tax = After-tax ÷ (1 − tax rate).
Week 4 · Balanced Scorecard — Translating strategy into measurable, balanced objectives
Four Perspectives (foundation → outcome)
Balanced ScorecardA strategic performance management tool (Kaplan & Norton, 1992) that balances financial and non-financial, leading and lagging indicators across four perspectives to give a complete view of strategy execution.
FinancialHow does the firm look to shareholders? Measures monetary outcomes — revenue growth, ROI, profit margins. Typically lagging.
CustomerHow do customers see the firm? Measures customer outcomes — NPS, satisfaction, retention rate, market share. Typically lagging.
Internal ProcessWhat must the firm excel at internally? Measures operational efficiency — cycle time, defect rate, order fulfilment accuracy.
Learning & GrowthCan the firm continue to improve? Measures capability building — training hours, staff turnover, IT system capability. The foundation enabling all other perspectives.
KPIKey Performance Indicator — a quantitative metric used to monitor progress toward a strategic objective. Must be specific, measurable, and linked to strategy.
PerspectiveQuestion it answersExample KPIs
FinancialHow do we look to shareholders?ROI, Revenue Growth, Cost Reduction
CustomerHow do customers see us?NPS, Satisfaction Score, Retention Rate
Internal ProcessWhat must we excel at?Order Cycle Time, Defect Rate
Learning & GrowthCan we continue to improve?Training Hours, Staff Turnover Rate
Causal chain (bottom → top)L&G enables → Processes improve → Customer outcomes better → Financial results follow
Leading vs Lagging Indicators
Leading IndicatorPredicts future performance (a CAUSE). Acts as an early warning signal — e.g. training hours predict future staff productivity. Enables proactive intervention.
Lagging IndicatorMeasures past outcomes (an EFFECT). Reports what has already happened — e.g. net profit, customer churn rate. Cannot be used to intervene before the result is locked in.
LeadingPredicts future performance (cause) — e.g. training hours
LaggingMeasures past outcomes (effect) — e.g. net profit, churn rate
Exam trapCustomer satisfaction is typically LAGGING — it results from past service delivery. Training hours are leading (they predict future productivity).
Strategy Map & Origin
Strategy MapA diagram showing cause-and-effect links between strategic objectives across all four BSC perspectives, making explicit how Learning & Growth investments ultimately drive Financial outcomes.
Strategy map = diagram of cause-and-effect links between objectives across all 4 perspectives
Example: Train staff (L&G) → faster service (Process) → higher satisfaction (Customer) → revenue growth (Financial)
Developed by Kaplan and Norton (1992, Harvard Business Review)
Fixes the weakness of purely financial reporting: lagging-only metrics miss non-financial value drivers
Dysfunctional Consequences of Performance Measures
Tunnel VisionDysfunctional consequence: over-focus on one metric causes neglect of equally important unmeasured areas (e.g. measuring only speed causes quality to suffer).
GamingDysfunctional consequence: manipulating the metric without improving the underlying performance (e.g. deferring R&D to hit a short-term profit target).
Tunnel VisionFocus on one metric → other important areas neglected (e.g. speed vs quality)
GamingManipulate the number without improving the real outcome (e.g. defer R&D to boost short-term profit)
MisrepresentationFalsify data when measurement is poorly monitored
Short-termismCut long-run investment to hit a quarterly financial target
Exam tipName the specific dysfunctional consequence AND explain the mechanism: “Measuring only calls-per-hour causes tunnel vision — agents rush calls, damaging quality which is not measured.”
Digital Transformation — 3 Economic Properties
Perfect TransmissionDigital info sent without loss or distortion across any distance at minimal cost
Indefinite ReplicationDigital products copied unlimited times with no degradation (unlike physical goods)
Near-Zero Marginal CostAfter initial investment, serving one more customer costs almost nothing → massive scalability
Why it mattersThese three properties explain why digital businesses scale without proportional cost increases — a software company with 1 million users has nearly the same marginal cost per user as one with 1,000.
Business Model Canvas (9 Blocks)
Business Model CanvasA strategic tool (Osterwalder) mapping how a firm creates, delivers, and captures value across 9 building blocks: Customer Segments, Value Propositions, Channels, Customer Relationships, Revenue Streams, Key Resources, Key Activities, Key Partnerships, Cost Structure.
Information CapitalBSC term for technology assets (IT systems, databases, infrastructure) that sit in the Learning & Growth perspective as a leading investment driving future process and financial outcomes.
BlockWhat it captures
Customer SegmentsWho are the customers?
Value PropositionsWhat value do we deliver?
ChannelsHow do we reach customers?
Customer RelationshipsHow do we maintain/grow customer relationships?
Revenue StreamsHow do we earn money?
Key ResourcesAssets required (physical, IP, human, financial)
Key ActivitiesCritical things the firm must do
Key PartnershipsWho helps us?
Cost StructureWhat does it cost to operate?
IT & the BMCIT investment can affect multiple blocks simultaneously — e.g. a CRM improves Customer Relationships + Channels; cloud infrastructure reduces Cost Structure; digital platforms transform Revenue Streams (one-off sales → subscriptions).
Platform vs Pipeline Business Models
Pipeline ModelA business model where value flows linearly: firm creates product → sells to customer (e.g. traditional retailer, taxi company).
Platform ModelA business model that facilitates interactions between two or more user groups, creating value through network effects (e.g. Uber, Airbnb, eBay).
Network EffectThe phenomenon where a platform becomes more valuable as more users join — e.g. Uber is more useful with more drivers AND more riders. Creates exponential value growth.
PipelineLinear: firm creates product → sells to customer (e.g. traditional taxi company)
PlatformFacilitates interactions between two+ user groups; value grows via network effects (e.g. Uber, Airbnb)
Network effect: the more users, the more valuable the platform — exponential value growth
Uber owns no cars; Airbnb owns no hotels — the platform coordinates, the users provide supply
Exam tipPipeline = produce + sell (linear flow). Platform = connect + coordinate (two-sided market). The key difference is WHERE value is created — inside the firm (pipeline) or between users (platform).
How to Approach W4 Exam Questions
Step-by-Step Guide

  2. Trace the chain: L&G enables → Processes improve → Customer outcomes better → Financial results follow
  3. Design BSC: give one specific objective + one measurable KPI per perspective; state a target value
  4. Label each indicator as leading (predicts future) or lagging (measures past) and explain why

Week 5 · Time Value of Money — A dollar today is worth more than a dollar in the future
Lump Sum
FV= PV × (1 + r)n
PV= FV ÷ (1 + r)n
r= (FV ÷ PV)1/n − 1
n= ln(FV ÷ PV) ÷ ln(1 + r)
APR vs EAR (Compounding)
EAR= (1 + APR ÷ m)m − 1
Periodic rate= APR ÷ m  (e.g. APR 12% monthly = 1% per month)
m = compounding periods per year (monthly=12, quarterly=4, semi-annual=2)
RuleEAR ≥ APR always. Equal only when m = 1 (annual compounding).
Annuities
Ordinary Annuity — payments at END of each period
PV= C × [1 − (1+r)−n] ÷ r
FV= C × [(1+r)n − 1] ÷ r
Variations
Annuity Due (payments at START): Ordinary PV × (1+r) → higher PV, each payment one period earlier
Deferred Annuity (delayed by k periods): Ordinary PV × (1+r)−k
Perpetuities
FlatPV = C ÷ r
GrowingPV = C1 ÷ (r − g) → C1 is the FIRST payment; requires r > g
Deferred perpetuity (first payment at year k+1)Step 1: PV at year k = C ÷ r   Step 2: discount back: PV0 = (C÷r) ÷ (1+r)k
How to Approach W5 Exam Questions
Step-by-Step Guide
  1. Identify what you're solving for: FV, PV, r, n, or C (payment amount)
  2. Classify the cash flow: single lump sum / ordinary annuity / annuity due / perpetuity / deferred / growing
  3. Match the right formula to the cash flow pattern
  4. Convert rates to the correct period: monthly rate = APR ÷ 12; quarterly = APR ÷ 4. Never mix annual rate with monthly periods
  5. Deferred annuity/perpetuity: solve as if starting now (PV at the period just before the first payment), then discount that value back to today
  6. Annuity due: multiply ordinary annuity PV by (1+r) — payments come one period earlier
  7. Show full working: write the formula, substitute values, compute step by step, state the final answer with units and rounding
Most Common Mistakes(1) Using annual rate with monthly periods — always convert. (2) Confusing annuity due vs ordinary: annuity due has payments at START of period. (3) Deferred perpetuity: discount at period k, not k+1 — PV is one period BEFORE the first payment.
Week 7 · Capital Budgeting — Evaluating whether investments create or destroy value
The 4 Main Methods
MethodWhat it measuresAccept rule
NPVDollar value added: CF0 + Σ[CFt ÷ (1+r)t]NPV > 0
IRR% return — rate where NPV = 0IRR > hurdle rate
PINPV ÷ |Initial Investment|PI > 0
PaybackYears to recover initial investmentWithin target period
EAA — Equivalent Annual Annuity
EAA= NPV × r ÷ [1 − (1+r)−n]
Use when comparing mutually exclusive projects with different lives
For cost comparison: choose the least negative EAA (lowest annual cost)
NPV vs IRR Conflict
Conflict arises for mutually exclusive projects differing in scale or timing
NPV = $ value added to firm → always the correct decision rule
IRR = % return; assumes reinvestment at IRR (often unrealistic)
RuleWhen NPV and IRR conflict — always trust NPV. It maximises firm value.
Decision Rules Summary
Independent projects: accept all with NPV > 0
Mutually exclusive, same life: choose highest NPV
Mutually exclusive, different lives: use EAA, choose highest
Cost comparison only: choose least negative EAA
How to Approach W7 Exam Questions
Step-by-Step Guide
  1. List all cash flows with their timing: Year 0 (outflow, negative), Years 1–n (inflows)
  2. Identify the discount rate (cost of capital or hurdle rate)
  3. Discount each cash flow: CFt ÷ (1+r)t
  4. NPV: sum all discounted CFs (CF0 + PV of inflows). Accept if NPV > 0
  5. Payback: track cumulative CFs year by year; fraction of year = remaining deficit ÷ that year's CF
  6. EAA: first calculate NPV, then EAA = NPV × r ÷ [1−(1+r)−n]. Use when lives differ
  7. Decision rule: state your conclusion (accept/reject) and the rule used. NPV is always the primary/correct rule
Most Common MistakeUsing IRR or payback to choose between mutually exclusive projects. ALWAYS use NPV as the final decision rule — it measures absolute dollar value added to the firm, which is what maximises shareholder value. IRR only tells you % return (ignores scale).
Week 8 · Valuation — Pricing bonds and equities using discounted cash flow models
Bond Pricing
PriceP0 = C × [1−(1+r)−n] ÷ r  +  F ÷ (1+r)n
C = periodic coupon = Face Value × coupon rate per period
F = face/par value  ·  r = YTM per period  ·  n = total periods
RelationshipPrice
Coupon rate > YTMPremium — P > Face Value
Coupon rate = YTMPar — P = Face Value
Coupon rate < YTMDiscount — P < Face Value
Semi-annual bondsr = APR ÷ 2  ·  n = years × 2  ·  C = annual coupon ÷ 2
Gordon Growth Model (DDM)
P0= D1 ÷ (r − g)
r (required)= D1 ÷ P0 + g
g (growth)= Retention Ratio × ROI = (1 − payout ratio) × ROI
If D0 was just paid: D1 = D0 × (1+g)
Exam trap“Just paid D0” means you must calculate D1 = D0 × (1+g) before using GGM.
How to Approach W8 Exam Questions
Bond Pricing Steps
  1. Identify: face value, coupon rate, YTM, maturity (years), compounding frequency
  2. Convert to period units: semi-annual → coupon ÷ 2; YTM ÷ 2; n × 2
  3. Apply formula: P = C × annuity factor + F × discount factor
  4. Sanity check: coupon rate > YTM → price > face (premium); coupon < YTM → price < face (discount)
Equity Valuation (GGM) Steps
  1. Determine D0 vs D1: “just paid” = D0 → must calculate D1 = D0 × (1+g)
  2. Calculate g if not given: g = Retention Ratio × ROI = (1 − payout ratio) × ROI
  3. Apply: P0 = D1 ÷ (r − g). Check r > g or formula fails
  4. Required return: r = D1 ÷ P0 + g (rearranged GGM)
Most Common MistakeConfusing D0 and D1. “Just paid dividend of $X” = D0 = $X → calculate D1 = X × (1+g). “Next dividend” or “expected dividend” = D1 → use directly. Getting this wrong costs the entire mark.
Week 9 · Risk & WACC — Pricing risk and computing the firm's cost of capital
Types of Risk
SystematicMarket-wide risk — cannot be diversified away (captured by β)
UnsystematicFirm-specific risk — eliminated through diversification
CAPM only compensates for systematic risk — unsystematic earns no extra return
CAPM & Beta
E[Ri]= Rf + βi × (E[RM] − Rf)
MRP= E[RM] − Rf  (Market Risk Premium)
β = 0 → E[R] = Rf  ·  β = 1 → moves with market  ·  β > 1 → more volatile  ·  β < 1 → less volatile
Over / UndervaluedRequired return (CAPM) > actual return → OVERVALUED. Required < actual → UNDERVALUED.
WACC
WACC= wE×RE + wP×RP + wD×RD×(1−TC)
V= MVE + MVP + MVD  ·  weights = MV component ÷ V
Always use market value weights (not book value)
Debt × (1−TC) because interest is tax-deductible
From D/E Ratio → Weights
wD= (D/E) ÷ (1 + D/E)  ·  wE = 1 ÷ (1 + D/E)
ExampleD/E = 0.50 → wD = 0.50÷1.50 = 0.333; wE = 1÷1.50 = 0.667
HPR
HPR= (P1 − P0 + Dividends) ÷ P0
How to Approach W9 Exam Questions
CAPM Steps
  1. Identify: Rf, E[RM] or MRP (= E[RM] − Rf), and β
  2. Apply CAPM: E[Ri] = Rf + β × MRP
  3. Solve for MRP: if a correctly-priced stock is given, rearrange — MRP = (Return − Rf) ÷ β
  4. Valuation check: required return (CAPM) vs actual/observed return — required > actual → OVERVALUED; required < actual → UNDERVALUED
WACC Steps
  1. Find total market value: V = MVE + MVP + MVD
  2. Compute weights: wE = MVE ÷ V; wP = MVP ÷ V; wD = MVD ÷ V
  3. After-tax cost of debt: RD × (1 − TC)
  4. Apply WACC: wE×RE + wP×RP + wD×RD×(1−TC)
  5. If D/E ratio given: wD = (D/E) ÷ (1 + D/E); wE = 1 ÷ (1 + D/E)
Most Common MistakeUsing book value weights instead of market value weights for WACC. Book value is historical; market value reflects what investors pay TODAY. Always use market values (share price × shares outstanding; bond market price × face value).
Week 10 · Integration — Connecting all course topics to long-run value creation
Value Creation Test
ROICReturn on Invested Capital — the return a firm earns on the capital deployed in its operations. Compared to WACC to determine whether value is created or destroyed.
WACCWeighted Average Cost of Capital — the minimum return a firm must earn to satisfy all capital providers (debt + equity). Acts as the hurdle rate in NPV analysis and the benchmark for ROIC.
Value CreationOccurs when ROIC > WACC — the firm earns more on its invested capital than it costs to finance. Equivalent to NPV > 0 at the project level.
Value DestructionOccurs when ROIC < WACC — capital is deployed in projects that do not cover their financing cost. Equivalent to NPV < 0.
Creates ValueROIC > WACC — firm earns more than cost of capital
Destroys ValueROIC < WACC — firm earns less than required
NPV > 0 means project return > WACC → same concept from W7 at firm level
WACC is the hurdle rate: only accept projects where return > WACC

Key Theories & Concepts

Stakeholder Theory (Freeman): Consider all stakeholders — employees, customers, community, environment
ESG: Poor Environmental, Social, Governance performance → higher cost of capital → lower firm value

Shareholder vs Stakeholder Theory
Shareholder Theory (Friedman)A firm's sole social responsibility is to maximise returns to shareholders, within legal and ethical rules. Social investment is shareholders' private choice, not the firm's duty.
Stakeholder Theory (Freeman)Firms have responsibilities to ALL stakeholders — employees, customers, suppliers, communities, environment — not just shareholders. Ignoring stakeholders creates long-run risks that destroy value.
ESGEnvironmental, Social, Governance — dimensions of non-financial risk. Poor ESG performance → higher perceived risk → higher WACC → fewer value-creating projects pass the NPV hurdle.
Corporate GovernanceThe system of rules, practices, and processes by which a company is directed and controlled. Determines how different stakeholders (shareholders, managers, employees) interact; shapes incentives, accountability, and long-run value creation.
FriedmanFirm's sole duty = maximise returns to shareholders. Social responsibility = shareholders' private choice, not firm's.
FreemanFirm must consider ALL stakeholders — employees, customers, suppliers, communities, environment.
For long-run value, stakeholder theory is more consistent: ignoring employees → turnover costs; ignoring customers → revenue erosion; ignoring community → regulatory/reputational risk
Strong stakeholder management → lower perceived risk → lower WACC → more value-creating projects
Exam tipDon't just say “stakeholder theory is better.” Trace the mechanism: poor ESG → higher perceived risk → higher WACC → fewer projects pass NPV hurdle → less long-run value.
CSFs vs KPIs
Critical Success Factor (CSF)A strategic area where the organisation MUST excel to achieve its goals. Qualitative priority — e.g. “patient safety” for a hospital. Defines WHAT matters.
KPIKey Performance Indicator — a quantitative measure monitoring progress toward a CSF. e.g. “hospital-acquired infections per 1,000 patient days.” Measures HOW WELL the CSF is being achieved.
CSFCritical Success Factor — a strategic area the organisation MUST excel at (qualitative priority)
KPIKey Performance Indicator — a quantitative measure that monitors progress toward the CSF
Hospital example: CSF = “patient safety”  →  KPI = “rate of hospital-acquired infections per 1,000 patient days”
CSFs define WHAT matters; KPIs measure HOW WELL those things are being managed
Without CSFs, KPIs have no strategic anchor. Without KPIs, CSFs cannot be monitored.
Three Types of Performance Measures
Financial MeasuresPerformance measures expressed in monetary terms (profit, ROI, revenue). Lagging — they show past outcomes but cannot predict future performance or capture how strategy is being executed.
Non-Financial MeasuresPerformance measures not expressed in money (customer satisfaction, defect rate, training hours). Often leading — they predict future financial outcomes by tracking operational and human drivers.
Quantitative MeasuresMeasures expressed as numbers, enabling comparison and benchmarking. Limitation: not everything important can be quantified (e.g. brand reputation, culture).
Qualitative MeasuresMeasures that capture important dimensions through description rather than numbers (e.g. management quality, brand strength). Subjective but captures what numbers miss.
Internal MeasuresTrack performance within the firm — process efficiency, cost control, cycle times. Limitation: do not capture whether value is actually being delivered to external stakeholders.
External MeasuresTrack stakeholder outcomes beyond the firm — customer satisfaction, market share, community impact. Capture whether internal processes translate into real-world value.

Integrated Report — 6 Capitals
Integrated ReportA report communicating how an organisation creates value over time across six capitals (financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social/relationship, natural) — beyond just financial profit.
An Integrated Report measures value creation across all six capitals:
1. FinancialFunds available (equity, debt, retained earnings)
2. ManufacturedPhysical infrastructure — buildings, equipment, technology
3. IntellectualKnowledge, IP, patents, systems, organisational know-how
4. HumanEmployee skills, motivation, loyalty, capability
5. Social & RelationshipCommunity relationships, reputation, brand, stakeholder trust
6. NaturalEnvironmental resources — water, land, biodiversity, carbon
Exam tipAn IR goes beyond profit to show value creation AND destruction across all capitals. A firm may create financial capital while destroying natural capital — the IR makes this visible.
Pieconomics — 3 Principles
PieconomicsFramework asking whether a firm's activity grows both the private AND social “pie.” Three tests: Multiplication ($1 cost → >$1 social benefit), Comparative Advantage (firm creates more benefit than alternatives), Materiality (benefits a significant stakeholder group).
Asks: does growing the firm's “pie” also grow the social pie?
1. MultiplicationDoes $1 of private cost create >$1 of social benefit?
2. Comparative AdvantageDoes $1 by the firm create more social benefit than $1 by government or a charity?
3. MaterialityDoes the activity benefit a materially significant group of stakeholders?
Activities satisfying ALL three justify the firm's profit and stakeholder investment simultaneously
Exam applicationUse pieconomics to evaluate CSR or social investment decisions. A firm hiring unemployed youth may pass all three tests: multiplication ($1 wage creates >$1 in productivity + social benefit), comparative advantage (firm trains better than government), materiality (large unemployed cohort).
How to Approach W10 Exam Questions
Step-by-Step Guide
  1. Identify which course topics the question draws from (W2 through W9)
  2. Value creation test: ROIC > WACC = value created; ROIC < WACC = value destroyed; ROIC = WACC = break even
  3. ESG mechanism: poor ESG → higher perceived risk → higher WACC → fewer projects pass NPV hurdle → less long-run value
  4. CSF vs KPI: CSF = what to excel at (qualitative); KPI = how to measure it (quantitative). Always link the KPI back to the CSF.
  5. 3 types of measures: financial/non-financial · quantitative/qualitative · internal/external — explain the limitation of relying on only one
  6. 6 Capitals: an IR captures value creation and destruction across financial, manufactured, intellectual, human, social, and natural capital
  7. Integration chain: CVP (W3) → cash flows → NPV discounted at WACC (W9) → ROIC vs WACC verdict
  8. Always conclude by explicitly linking back to “long-run value creation” for shareholders or stakeholders
Exam TipW10 SA questions reward cross-topic integration. Name tools explicitly: “CVP (W3) reveals operating margins feeding cash flow projections, discounted at WACC (W9) via NPV (W7), producing ROIC we compare to WACC to test value creation.” Generic answers without explicit cross-topic linkage earn minimal marks.
